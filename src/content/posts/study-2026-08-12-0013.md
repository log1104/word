# Freedom from Sin's Dominion

**You:** Study Romans 6:14

**AI:** In Romans 6:14, the Apostle Paul makes a definitive declaration regarding the believer's relationship to sin: _"For sin shall not be your master, because you are not under law, but under grace."_ (BSB)

This verse serves as the foundation for Christian sanctification. It explains why a believer is no longer required to live in bondage to sinful patterns.

### The End of Sin’s Tyranny

Paul uses the word **master** ([Strong's G2961](https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/2961.htm) - _kyrieuō_), which means to exercise lordship or rule over someone. Before coming to Christ, every human being is a slave to sin ([John 8:34](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/John/8/34)). 

The statement "sin shall not be your master" is not a suggestion or a command; it is a statement of fact based on the believer's union with Christ. Because the believer has died to sin in Christ and been raised to new life ([Romans 6:4](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/6/4)), sin has lost its legal right to command the believer's will. It may still entice or deceive, but it can no longer compel.

### Why Law Cannot Conquer Sin

Paul provides the reason for this freedom: "because you are not **under law** ([Strong's G3551](https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/3551.htm) - _nomos_)." 

To be "under law" means to be under a system where one's standing before God is determined by their ability to obey commandments. While the Law is holy and good, it lacks the power to change the human heart. In fact, the Law actually exposes and arouses sin ([Romans 7:5](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/7/5)). If you are "under law," sin will always be your master because the Law can only condemn the sinner; it cannot empower the sinner to be righteous. 

As long as a person is under the Law, they are under the "ministry of death" ([2 Corinthians 3:7](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/2%20Corinthians/3/7)), where sin’s mastery remains unbroken.

### The Dominion of Grace

Instead of being under the Law, the believer is "under **grace** ([Strong's G5485](https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/5485.htm) - _charis_)." 

Grace is often defined as unmerited favor, but in the context of Romans 6, it is also a powerful reign or dominion. To be "under grace" means:

1. **Acceptance**: You are already accepted by God through the work of Christ, not your own performance.
2. **Empowerment**: Grace provides the internal strength of the Holy Spirit to do what the Law could only demand from the outside ([Galatians 5:18](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Galatians/5/18)).
3. **New Identity**: You are no longer a slave to the old master, but a child of the new Master.

Grace does not lead to lawlessness. On the contrary, Paul argues that being under grace is the _only_ way to actually defeat sin. When the motive for obedience shifts from fear of punishment (Law) to the power of a new life and the love of God (Grace), the mastery of sin is effectively shattered.

### Summary of Key Connections

*   **The Power Shift**: Sin's lordship is replaced by Christ's lordship. See [Romans 6:12](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/6/12), where Paul warns not to let sin reign in your mortal body.
*   **The Legal Shift**: We are "dead to the law" so that we can belong to another—Jesus Christ. See [Romans 7:4](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/7/4).
*   **The Source of Truth**: Law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ ([John 1:17](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/John/1/17)).

Paul is not suggesting that being under grace results in automatic sinless perfection, but rather a change in who has legal authority over your life.

### Grace as a Governing Power

In this context, **grace** ([Strong's G5485](https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/5485.htm) - _charis_) is not merely a "forgiveness blanket." It is a dynamic, governing reality. 

When Paul says you are "under" ([Strong's G5259](https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/5259.htm) - _hypo_) grace, he is using the language of jurisdiction. Just as a citizen is "under" the laws of a nation, the believer is under the "reign" of grace. As Paul says earlier in [Romans 5:21](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/5/21), "so that... grace might reign through righteousness."

### The Power of the Spirit

You are correct to link grace with the power of the Spirit. While Paul doesn't use the word "Spirit" in this specific verse, he spends [Romans 8](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/8/1) explaining exactly _how_ grace functions: through "the law of the Spirit of life" ([Romans 8:2](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/8/2)).

The Law (the Old Covenant) could tell you what was right, but it provided no power to do it. Grace, however, provides the **Holy Spirit**, who:

1.  **Writes the Law on the heart**: ([Hebrews 8:10](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Hebrews/8/10)).
2.  **Empowers obedience**: "For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live" ([Romans 8:13](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/8/13)).

### Does "Under Grace" Mean We Will Not Sin?

We must be careful with the phrase "we will not sin." Paul’s argument is subtle:

1.  **Sin’s Mastery is Broken**: Paul says "sin shall not be your **master**" ([Strong's G2961](https://biblehub.com/strongs/greek/2961.htm) - _kyrieuō_). This means sin no longer has the _right_ to command you. You are no longer a slave who must obey.
2.  **The Presence of Sin Remains**: If grace made sinning impossible, Paul would not have to command the believers in [Romans 6:12](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/6/12) to "not let sin reign." You do not give commands to people who are incapable of doing the opposite.
3.  **The Possibility of Sinning**: In the very next verse ([Romans 6:15](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/6/15)), Paul asks, "Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace?" If grace made sin impossible, this question would be irrelevant.

### Summary: The Freedom to Obey

Paul is saying that under the Law, you were a slave to sin and had no choice but to fail. Under Grace (by the power of the Spirit), the chains are broken. You are now **free to not sin**. 

*   **Under Law**: Sin is your master; you are "bound" to fail ([Romans 7:5](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/7/5)).
*   **Under Grace**: The Spirit provides the "enablement" ([Galatians 5:16](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Galatians/5/16)). If you walk by the Spirit, you will not gratify the desires of the flesh. 

The "will not" in [Romans 6:14](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/Romans/6/14) refers to **dominion**. Sin will not "lord it over you." It may still trip you up, and you may still struggle ([1 John 1:8](https://bible-xref.247base.uk/1%20John/1/8)), but it can never again claim ownership of you or force your ultimate allegiance. Grace has transferred you into a new kingdom where sin is a defeated usurper, not a rightful king.
